Partsbar 11 · 0:24

Every part of every song is one call away.

Import a folder of stems and StageTracks finds the beat grid, the key and the parts. The part names come from your guide track: when it says “verse” or “chorus”, that becomes a marker you can jump to.

On stage the biggest thing on the screen is what comes next, and when. Press GO, or queue a jump and let it fire on time.

Jump on the grid
Choose when a jump lands: now, on the next beat, on the next bar or at the end of the section. It always lands on the grid.
Loop a part
Hold the part that is playing for as long as you need it, then let go.
The guide speaks first
The guide track announces the next part one bar before it arrives.
Segue between songs
A pad in the song’s key carries the room from one song to the next, and the click picks up at the new tempo.
Voicebar 27 · 1:02

Say it, and the jump is queued.

Arm the microphone and the musical director can call the show: “let’s go to the chorus”, “second verse”, “go to the bridge”. StageTracks queues the jump and shows it as STANDBY until it fires.

Speech is recognised on the Mac, in English and Norwegian at once. A misheard word is matched by sound against the parts this song really has, and a command can only pick one of those parts or one of a few fixed commands.

Tracksbar 40 · 1:34

Every stem gets a lane. Cut it, move it, rearrange it.

Stems are routed to output groups by file name, and a mistake is fixed in the lane list. Turn on Edit to cut a track, move a piece, trim an edge or repeat a part on every track at once. Nothing is written to your audio files, and ⌘Z takes it back.

Also in the box

Transpose
Move a song up or down by semitones. The chord chart follows.
Click and pad
A built-in click and a pad that holds in the song’s key, each on its own output.
Timecode
LTC timecode on its own channel, following the song.
MIDI and keys
Learn any control from a MIDI pad or a key on the keyboard.
Setlist
Reorder by dragging, import from folders, export as PDF, text or spreadsheet, or start a new one.

Mashups without a stop

Build a new song from parts of several songs, in the order you choose. Match the tempo and the key, and every part becomes a marker, so jumping from one song’s chorus to another’s verse never stops.

Remotebar 61 · 2:24

Your phone or iPad is the second screen.

StageTracks serves its own web page on your Wi-Fi. Open it in Safari or Chrome: no app to install, no internet needed.

Setlist
The songs and their parts, with the time left. Tap a song or a part to go there.
Perform
Big part buttons, GO, loop, pad, click and segue.
Lyrics
Words and chords that scroll with the song.

Several devices can connect at once and always agree. Add a PIN when the Wi-Fi is shared.

Words, chords and plans

Lyrics and chords come from LRCLIB, from a text, ChordPro or LRC file, or from Planning Center, where a plan’s songs, charts, keys and tempos can be pulled in. The chords are moved to the key you play in.

  1. Open the disk image

    Double-click StageTracks.dmg and drag StageTracks into Applications.

  2. Open it once, past the macOS warning

    StageTracks is not notarised by Apple yet, so macOS says it cannot check the app.

    macOS 15 or later: double-click StageTracks and press Done. Open System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and press Open Anyway. macOS 14: Control-click the app, choose Open, then Open. You do this once.

  3. Allow the microphone

    Voice commands need the microphone and speech recognition. macOS asks once; the rest of the app works without them.

Prefer Terminal? Remove the quarantine flag instead:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/StageTracks.app

Why the warning?

Notarisation needs a paid Apple developer account, which this beta does not have yet. To check that the file is the one published here, compare its SHA-256 with the line above:

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/StageTracks.dmg
Questionsbar 87 · 3:26

Questions

Is it free?

Yes, while it is in beta. What happens after the beta will be announced here.

Does it run on an Intel Mac?

No. This build is for Apple Silicon (M1 or later) and needs macOS 14 or later.

Which audio interface can I use?

Any CoreAudio interface, with any number of outputs. StageTracks talks to the device directly, so a 16-channel USB interface gets 16 outputs. If a device misbehaves, the Outputs tab can copy an audio report for you to send along.

Which files can I import?

A folder of stems per song (WAV, AIFF, CAF, MP3, M4A or FLAC), a folder of song folders, or drop them on the window. Stems are routed by file name: Click, Guide, Synth Bass, BGVs and so on.

Do I need a guide track?

No, but with one the parts are named for you; each spoken call is read on the Mac. Without it the parts get neutral names you can change, and markers can be placed by hand.

Which languages does voice control understand?

English and Norwegian at once. On macOS 26 English uses Apple’s newer speech analyzer, which copes better with noise.

Is the app in Norwegian?

The app is in English. Voice control understands Norwegian, and this page comes in both languages.

Does anything leave my Mac?

Audio and speech stay on the Mac. Lookups you ask for, such as lyrics from LRCLIB or your plans from Planning Center, go to those services. If you store your own Claude API key for the optional language-model fallback, the phrase is sent to that service for a decision when the rules cannot decide.

Does it work with Planning Center?

Yes. Sign in from the Planning Center tab to see your services and plans, take a plan’s chart, key and tempo into a song, or add a plan’s song as a pad and click loop. Signing in needs an API client ID that you create for your own Planning Center account.

Something is wrong. Where do I report it?

Open an issue on GitHub. Say which macOS you run and what you did; the Outputs tab can copy an audio report for audio problems.
